WHY I BUILT THIS

The systems that save businesses can save families too.

I have spent 16 years going under the hood of businesses, finding what only lives in someone's head and building the systems that let it live somewhere else. What I realized is that the same problem destroying businesses is also destroying families.

My sister is a full-time caregiver for her son, who cannot walk, talk, or eat. Everything he needs lives in her head. Her husband knows pieces. My parents know less. If something happened to her tomorrow, the people who love her son most would not know how to care for him the way she does.

My husband is a veteran of the U.S. Air Force. Before he left the service, I used to have nightmares about what would happen if they knocked on my door. Even now, I ask him: what is the mortgage login? Where are the insurance documents? What happens to our boys if something happens to us?

You cannot have peace if the system is still in your brain and nowhere else. That is true for a business. It is just as true for a life.

"CAN'T I JUST DO THIS MYSELF?"

A list is a start. It is not a system.

You could write all of this down yourself. Most people begin to, then stall, because getting it out of your head is the easy part. The structure, the security, and the handoff are what actually make it work when someone needs it.

01
The questions you don't think to ask.
You will write down where the mortgage is. You will forget the two-factor code that goes to your phone, the backup payment method, and the one button that breaks every month. I know what a complete SOP needs because I have built thousands. The gaps you can't see are the ones that cause the crisis.
02
Built, stored, and actually findable.
A pile of notes is not a system. Someone has to turn it into clear, repeatable instructions, then store them somewhere your family can find, follow, and trust while they are under stress. Recording it, organizing it, and making it usable is the difference between information and a plan.
03
Secure, and seen only by the right people.
Not everything belongs in the same place. Passwords should not live loose in a document. Some people get the recipes, not the financial accounts. Who has access, what stays in a protected vault, and what gets shared is designed in from the start, not bolted on after.

These are a few of the considerations we built into Part A and Part B. The emotional weight is real. So is the structure underneath it.

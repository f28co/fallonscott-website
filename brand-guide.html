Do

Set in Fraunces 600. Keep the period accent (#B45309). Maintain x-height of clear space on all sides. Never let nav or imagery touch the wordmark. Pair only with the approved color grounds.

Don't

Don't recolor the period. Don't add a tagline lockup beneath it. Don't substitute a sans-serif. Don't stretch, italicize the wordmark, or apply effects (shadow, outline, gradient).

05 / Voice + Tone

Direct. Operator-grade. Warm without sentiment.

A friend who used to do internal audit. Specific over abstract. Names what's broken without performing concern. Italic clauses for the parts that matter, never bold, never exclaimed.

01

Plain-spoken, never preachy

The brand says what it means. No jargon, no consultant-ese, no thought-leader trance. Short sentences carry weight; long ones earn their length.

"You can't delegate clarity you don't have."

02

Specific over abstract

Numbers, names, sequence. Whenever a sentence could go vague or concrete, it goes concrete. "Collapse 28 steps to 20," not "streamline operations."

"Give me 20 minutes and I'll find you $20,000 of operational inefficiency."

03

Diagnoses, then directs

Always names the symptom before naming the fix. The reader should feel seen first, sold to second. We earn the prescription.

"Most founders don't have a hiring problem. They have a system problem."

04

Italic for emphasis only

The italicized clause is the brand's only audible inflection. One per paragraph, two per page. It is the equivalent of a hand on the table, not a pointed finger.

"Build the system before you spend a dollar on payroll."

Voice in practice

Do say

  • "Three steps to delegation. Most founders skip the first two."
  • "Give me 20 minutes and I'll find you $20,000 of inefficiency."
  • "The business lives in your head, and you can feel the weight of it."
  • "Build the system before you spend a dollar on payroll."
  • "Operators don't decorate. They install."

No em dashes. Use periods or semicolons. Em dashes signal AI-written copy.

Lead client-facing language with the outcome, not the audit. Externally, lead with "architect before automate." The audit lives inside the architect step; reveal it second, never as the opening pitch.

Lead with the story, then the framework. Stories sell. State the principle after the reader has already seen themselves in the example.

Don't say

  • "Unlock your business potential with proven strategies."
  • "We partner with visionary leaders to deliver outcomes."
  • "Transformative. Synergistic. Best-in-class. Holistic."
  • "Empower your team to thrive in today's landscape!"
  • "Let's hop on a discovery call to see if we're a fit 🚀"

Tone by surface

Hero / Manifesto

Decisive. Quotable. Italic-led. Short, weighted sentences.

Pain / Diagnosis

Plain, almost flat. Naming, not selling. The reader should recognize themselves.

Methodology / How

Sequential. Numbered. Operator-grade. No metaphor unless it earns its keep.

Pricing / CTA

Direct. Receipted. Numbers in mono. No exclamation points. No urgency theater.

Bio / About

First-person, restrained. Credentials as proof, not posture. CPA. Lean Six Sigma. Built it.

Email / DM

Short paragraphs. One ask per message. Plain greetings. No "I hope this finds you well."

05.5 / The core job

Simplify. Streamline. Connect.

Before the core job, there is the mission: Built to Last. Every simplification, every streamlined process, every connected system is in service of businesses and lives that outlast the people who built them. This is the most basic truth of the work; every other message is a more specific version of it.

— The job in one sentence

Fallon simplifies, streamlines, and connects the way a business runs, end to end, from how a customer first hears about you through to how you keep them.

— Simplify · the steps

Does this step need to exist at all? Eliminate the unnecessary.

— Streamline · the path

What's the straightest line from A to B? Remove the zigzag.

— Connect · the wiring

Do the systems talk, or is a human the switchboard operator? End the silos.

Reframes locked into the brand language

Chaos vs inefficiency

You don't have to be drowning to be in danger

Chaos is loud. Inefficiency is quiet, and it's the bigger market because those founders don't know they need help. If you're just fine, so is your competition. Fine is not a moat.

The switchboard operator

You're paying people to be software

Disconnected systems get bridged by a human plugging wires by hand all day. The fix isn't one platform for everything; it's the data flowing between the right tools without a person in the middle.

Lean Six Sigma · waste

Don't document or automate waste

You can't automate your way out of an un-optimized process. Document a disjointed one and your SOP library doubles. Automate 28 steps that should be 20 and you pay to execute waste faster. Simplify and connect first.

The scale warning

Small leak, big flood

Inefficiency doesn't stay the size it is today. A $20K/year leak becomes a $200K structural problem at scale; it survives growth and gets carried up the curve. Fix it while it's a checkup, not open-heart surgery.

The differentiator

Optimize inside a step

Most consultants collapse 28 steps to 20. Good ones say which to automate. Almost nobody opens a single step and optimizes inside it. You can only do that by having lived inside it.

The confidence line

$20K in 20 minutes

Give me 20 minutes and a description of how your business runs, and I can find you $20,000 of operational inefficiency. It scales monthly or annually by company size.

— How it relates to the frameworks

The core job (simplify, streamline, connect) is the what. The 4A Framework is the how. "Architect before automate" is the client-facing positioning. The 7 Stages of the Client Journey is the structure used in the audit. The why is freedom: fundable, scalable, sellable, so the founder is free.

— Pillar 01 · The why

Legacy and business continuity.

"Documentation is not administrative housekeeping. It is the act of making sure the work was worth it."

The emotional anchor. Key-person risk. The grandfather who built a dump-truck company that helped pave Houston, and watched it die with him because the secret sauce was never written down. The military spouse. The medical mom. Knowledge transfer is the prerequisite to delegation, delegation is the prerequisite to continuity, and continuity is the prerequisite to Built to Last. This is the pillar every other pillar serves.

Proof · the grandfather story · key-person risk · The Life Brief · "built to outlast burnout, the sale, and you"
— Pillar 02 · Method

Diagnose first: audit before architecture.

"The audit always comes before the architecture."

You cannot fix what you have not diagnosed. The audit comes before the architecture, every time. Internally this is the language of the method: audit before architect. Client-facing, it leads as architect before automate. Same sequence, two registers.

Proof · the 7 Stages of the Customer Journey · the diagnostic-first intake · AI Assessment
— Pillar 03 · Method

Knowledge transfer is the prerequisite.

"The wealth transfer only happens if the knowledge transfer happens first."

More human and more visual than "documentation." Knowledge transfer is what is actually happening when a founder writes a process down: they move what lives in their head into a system, a hire, an AI, a buyer, a family member. If you do not transfer the knowledge, no one can carry it forward.

Proof · the SOP Suite · the exit-ready seller · "your SOPs are your AI's training data"
— Pillar 04 · Method

Architect before automate.

"Automation on a broken process is not efficiency. It is amplified chaos."

The external-facing sequence that protects clients from expensive mistakes. Tools come last. The foundation comes first. Most AI help wires automation before anyone diagnosed the business or drew the spec; an architect decides what scales, what's safe, what connects, and what it should cost before a single tool is touched.

Proof · the 4A Framework · integrator-vs-architect · AI Integration page
— Pillar 05 · Method

Humans and AI work together by design.

"Humans and AI work together by design. Not as a compromise."

Not a hedge, a design principle. AI handles what is repeatable. Humans handle what requires judgment. The work is figuring out which is which and building the bridge between them, so people do their highest-value thinking and the machine carries the rest.

Proof · AI Systems Architecture · the risk-aware lens · "AI where it earns its place, humans where they belong"
— Pillar 06 · The number

The manual tax.

"What is the manual tax costing you?"

The objective math of what doing it by hand actually costs. Delivered like a neutral auditor revealing a number, not a coach hyping a transformation. It is the bridge from emotional to rational: once the cost is on the table, the decision makes itself.

Proof · the ROI calculator · "$20K in 20 minutes" · the AI Assessment
— Pillar 07 · The toolkit

Frameworks: the F28 method.

"Frameworks make complexity actionable."

The named systems that turn the beliefs into steps anyone can follow. Dump, Document, Delegate. The SSSSS framework (Stuck, Screen, SOP, Skill, Schedule). The 6-Step Optimization. The 7 Stages of the Customer Journey. The 4A Framework (Audit, Architect, Approve, Automate). A framework is how a conviction becomes a deliverable.

The 4A Framework · the method, named

— Audit. Architect. Approve. Automate.

Four steps to a successful integration. An A is shaped like a 4.

— 1 · Audit

What's actually there. The 7 Stages of the Client Journey; leadership and the people doing the work.

— 2 · Architect

The plan. Current state vs future state, designed around the vision and the P&L, risk mitigated before it's built in.

— 3 · Approve

The mockup. Stakeholders sign off before anything is built. This is what prevents the adoption problem.

— 4 · Automate

The build. AI where it earns its place, automation where AI is overkill, documented so it runs without you.

Adoption is not a fifth A. It's the outcome Approve protects. Keep it at four.

Two phrases, same sequence. "Audit before architect" is the internal, methodology language; "architect before automate" is the client-facing lead. It earns the nod instead of the wince, and the audit lives inside the architect step.

Three concentric circles. The brand speaks loudest to the inner circle. Outer-circle audiences encounter the brand through the SOP Suite, content, and referrals.

— Tier 01 · Primary

The bottlenecked founder

  • $1M–$10M revenue, service-based or product-led
  • Founder-led, 3–25 employees
  • Has hired and been burned by it
  • Cannot take a real vacation; the business lives in their head
  • Knows they need systems, not sure how
  • Wants to fund, scale, or sell what they built
  • Buys: diagnostic, fractional COO engagement

The operator inside the company

  • COO, integrator, OBM, head of ops
  • Brought in to fix what the founder couldn't
  • Buys the SOP Suite to install themselves
  • Influences the engagement decision
  • Becomes a referral source
  • Buys: SOP Suite, AI engagements

The peer, the platform, and the integrator

  • Other fractional COOs, OBMs, consultants
  • Podcasts, conferences, peer founders

  • Refers, partners, cites the brand as gold-standard

09 / Buyer tiers

Four buyers. Four stages.

The brand speaks to four buyers at four stages. The stage matters more than the revenue number. A $5M company with no SOPs is a Foundations buyer. A $500K company preparing for acquisition is an Exit-Ready Seller.

— Tier 01 · Foundations buyer · pre-$1M to $3M

Grew faster than the foundation

  • Built on hustle and loyalty; the first real hires exposed every gap the original team papered over
  • Pain: new hires can't be trained because nothing is written down
  • Hook: "Your business grew faster than the foundation underneath it."
  • Buys: SOP Suite, Foundations engagement
— Tier 02 · Optimization buyer · $3M to $15M

Systems built for a smaller company

  • Has some systems; they were built for a smaller version of the business
  • Pain: 30 people and still the bottleneck. AI tools that don't work. The same problems every quarter
  • Hook: "You have 30 people. You still can't take 30 days off. That's not a team problem."
  • Buys: Optimization engagement, AI Systems Architecture
— Tier 03 · Investor-ready buyer · $10M+

Defending operations to outside eyes

  • Preparing for a funding round or defending operations in diligence
  • Pain: investors want to see systems, not heroics. Due diligence reveals the gaps
  • Hook: "Fundable is not a feeling. It is a standard."
  • Buys: Fractional COO engagement
— Tier 04 · Exit-ready seller · any revenue

Transferring, not defending

  • Built something real over many years; ready to sell, but everything lives in their head
  • Pain: about to leave money on the table because the business can't run without them
  • Hook: "The wealth transfer only happens if the knowledge transfer happens first."
  • The distinction: not defending operations to get funded, transferring them to get paid. Selling as-is versus selling with renovations
  • Buys: SOP Suite, Fractional COO engagement, exit preparation

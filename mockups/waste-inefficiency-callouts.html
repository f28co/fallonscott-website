Two callouts, a few treatments each

Top group: "don't document or automate waste." Bottom group: "you don't have to be drowning to be in danger" (chaos vs inefficiency). Pick one treatment from each, or mix. Each is shown at full homepage width.

Callout 1 · Don't automate waste
Option A · Chocolate banner (loud, section-scale)
— Architect before automate

Don't document waste. Don't pay to automate it.

You can't automate your way out of a broken process. Document a disjointed one and your SOP library doubles. Automate the steps that shouldn't exist, and you just pay to run waste faster. We simplify and connect first, then automate what's left.

Option B · Three-beat cream (structured, explanatory)
— The Lean Six Sigma lens

Automation makes a process faster. It doesn't make it right.

01 · Document

Document a disjointed process and you just get a bigger pile of disjointed SOPs.

02 · Automate

Automate steps that shouldn't exist and you pay to run waste faster.

03 · Architect

Simplify and connect first. Then automate what actually earns its place.

An auditor's first question isn't "how do we speed this up?" It's "does this step need to exist at all?"

Option C · Compact pull-quote (quietest, slots between sections)

Don't automate waste. Simplify and connect first.

Most "AI rollouts" automate a broken process. You end up paying to run the mess faster. The audit comes before the build for a reason.

Callout 2 · You don't have to be drowning
Option A · Navy full callout (reaches the "we're fine" founder)
— A note for the "we're fine" founder

You don't have to be drowning to be in danger.

Chaos is loud, so it gets fixed. Inefficiency is quiet, so it gets carried, up the curve, into the raise, into the sale. If you're just fine, so is your competition. And fine is not a moat.

Option B · Loud vs quiet, two columns (makes the idea visual)
— Chaos vs inefficiency

The dangerous problems are the quiet ones.

Loud · chaos

You know it's broken.

Fires, dropped balls, the 11pm scramble. It hurts, but at least you fix it, because you can feel it.

Quiet · inefficiency

You think you're fine.

It still works, so nobody touches it. Then it scales with you, into the raise, into the sale, and quietly caps what the business is worth.

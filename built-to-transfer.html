Why your score looks like this

Four areas decide the size of the check you walk away with when you sell, hand off, or step back.

01 / Owner and key-person dependency

Can it run without you?

It isn't only about you. If the knowledge lives in your head, or in the two or three people who've been there forever, that's key-person risk. A buyer is inheriting the risk of replacing them. The work is turning individual knowledge into institutional knowledge.

02 / Transferability

Is it written down?

Buyers need documented workflows, clear decision rights, and people who can run the work without you standing over them. If it's all in your head, it isn't transferable.

03 / Revenue quality

Will the money keep coming?

Two things move this. Customer concentration: if one client is a big share of revenue, losing them guts the business. And recurring revenue: contracts, retainers, and repeat customers are worth far more than one-time jobs, because a buyer can count on them continuing.

04 / Financial readiness

Are the books clean?

If a buyer can't clearly see what the business earns and spends, they slow down, get suspicious, or walk. Clean books are the price of being taken seriously.

Why now

Baby boomers own millions of the small businesses in this country. Over the next decade, most of them will try to sell, hand off, or close. Economists call it the silver tsunami, the largest transfer of business ownership we have ever seen.

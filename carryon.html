How CarryOn works

Talk it out. We'll organize it for you.

No blank page, no forms to dread. Three quiet steps turn what's in your head into a guide anyone you trust can follow.

Capture

Just start talking.

CarryOn asks gentle questions and you answer out loud, the way you'd explain it to a friend. The recipe, the routine, the thing only you know how to handle. No typing, no structure to figure out.

Record when it comes to you, waiting in the pickup line, folding laundry, lying awake at 2am. A minute here, a minute there.

Voice-first · Video when it helps
Curate

We turn it into something they can follow.

CarryOn listens and organizes what you share into clear, findable sections like household, medical, and the people you care for, so nothing lives loose in a notebook or lost in your head.

Start anywhere. Record what feels most urgent. It fills in over time, and it's always there to add to.

Who CarryOn is for

Anyone whose loved ones depend on knowledge that only lives in them.

The caregiver
The medical parent or caregiver.
Medications, dosages, specialists, what soothes them, how they communicate. When all of it lives in one person, the people counting on you are one hard day away from crisis. CarryOn gets it out, gently, in your own words.
The military spouse
The one holding it together at home.
Before a deployment, someone needs to know how the household actually runs: the routines, the kids' schedules, the things you never had to explain. CarryOn is the handoff before the goodbye.
Planning ahead
The person facing a hard diagnosis.
Planning ahead isn't giving up hope. It's giving peace. Getting what's in your head into a place your people can reach, so they have what they need no matter what comes. You keep fighting. They stay held.
The one who runs it all
Anyone carrying the whole household in their head.
The appointments, the birthdays, the maintenance, the thousand small things. If everything slows down when you slow down, you're a single point of failure, and the people you love deserve a backup that isn't just you.

Why we built CarryOn

The systems that keep businesses alive can help the people we love carry on, too.

I've spent 16 years going under the hood of businesses, finding what only lives in one person's head and building the systems that let it live somewhere else. Somewhere along the way I realized the same thing quietly breaking companies is breaking the people we love.

My sister is a full-time caregiver for her son, who cannot walk, talk, or eat. Almost everything he needs lives in her head. If something happened to her tomorrow, the people who love him most wouldn't know how to care for him the way she does.

My husband is a veteran of the U.S. Air Force. For years I'd lie awake imagining uniformed officers at our door with a folded flag, and everything he carried in his head gone with him. CarryOn is the answer I wished I'd had: a way to get it all out, in your own voice, before you ever need it.

You can't have peace if the whole system still lives in your brain and nowhere else. That's true for a business. It's just as true for a life.

Fallon Scott
